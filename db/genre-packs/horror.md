# Investigative horror

Ordinary people find that the world is not what it seemed. The mood is unease that builds slowly; the truth is dangerous to know.

- People: witnesses who saw too little, experts who know too much, officials who want it closed, relatives who want answers. Most are frightened and some are lying for small human reasons.
- Places: archives, boarding houses, hospitals, lonely farms, a room that has been locked for years. One wrong detail does more than a list of horrors.
- Trouble: a mystery with a trail of clues. Every scene should offer at least one clue that leads on, and the core clues must be findable without luck. Suggest what happens if the investigators do nothing.
- Rewards: answers, a life saved, time bought. Knowledge has a price.
- Style: restrained. Describe evidence and its effect on people rather than the thing itself. Keep the GM-only truth separate from what the investigators can see.
