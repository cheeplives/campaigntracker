# Campaign Chronicle — RPG campaign tracker

A web-based tracker for tabletop campaigns: in-game calendar kept in four shifts, two shifts (Day and Night) or hours a day, weather by
region and season, moon phases, visibility, holidays, events with secret GM entries, player notes,
and a travel planner for overland, orbital and interstellar trips.

PHP 8 + MySQL on the server, plain JavaScript in the browser. No build step or frameworks.

Players sign in with **Google or GitHub**, the same way as the Sinless app on the same domain, and new accounts wait for an admin to approve them.

## Install

The full runbook (OAuth apps, config fields, upgrading an older install) is in [docs/DEPLOY.md](docs/DEPLOY.md). In short:

1. **Create a MySQL database** in your host's panel, or reuse the Sinless database so the two apps share accounts and one approval queue (fresh installs only).
2. **Deploy with git:** `git clone https://github.com/cheeplives/campaigntracker.git chronicle` inside your web root. Updates are `./deploy.sh` (pull, PHP syntax check, database upgrade).
3. **Configure:** copy `api/config.example.php` to `~/chronicle-config.php` (above the web root; `api/config.php` also works) and fill in the database, `base_url`, the Google/GitHub client ids and secrets, and your email under `admin_identities`.
4. **Add the callback** `<base_url>/api/auth/callback.php` to your Google OAuth client (as a second redirect URI next to Sinless's) and register a GitHub OAuth app for it.
5. **Install:** run `php tools/migrate.php` in the app folder to create the tables. (Or set `'allow_install' => true`, open `<base_url>/install.php` and press *Install or upgrade*, then set it back to `false`.) Later upgrades happen by themselves: `./deploy.sh` runs `tools/migrate.php` after each pull, backing up the database first.
6. **Sign in** with the email in `admin_identities`. You're approved as admin straight away; everyone else appears under *Users* to approve.

## Using it

- **Install as an app** (Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers): the install icon at the right of the address bar, or *Install Campaign Chronicle* in the browser's menu, adds it to the Start menu, dock or home screen and opens it in its own window. On iPhone and iPad, Safari's *Share → Add to Home Screen* does the same. It's still the website, signed in the same way and always up to date; with no connection it shows a short offline notice.
- **Users** (admins only): approve or revoke people after their first sign-in. Accounts from before Google/GitHub sign-in show as *not linked yet*; enter the player's email and their first sign-in with it takes over the old account and its campaigns.
- **Your account** (your name in the top bar): *Units* is one choice for everything the app converts: temperatures, travel distances and speeds, snow depth, rainfall and wind speed. *Imperial* shows °F, miles, inches and mph; *Metric* °C, km, cm and km/h; *None* shows each campaign in the units its GM picked (a campaign in °F or °R reads as Imperial, any other as Metric). It only changes what you see, and a campaign set to hide temperatures keeps them hidden. Below it, *Your campaigns* lists every campaign you belong to with your role (GM or Player) and a box for your character's name in each, the same name the Members tab shows; it saves when you leave the box.
- **New campaign:** pick a preset (Gregorian for the Northern or Southern Hemisphere, Imperial Calendar, Middle-earth, Glorantha, Forgotten Realms (Calendar of Harptos), Eberron (Galifar Calendar), Exandria, Golarion (Absalom Reckoning), Star Wars (Galactic Standard Calendar), Starship clock, or a blank custom calendar, which also takes its months and a week of any length with its own day names), a starting date, and the seasons. Each season has a name, a climate (spring, summer, autumn, winter or none) and a first month and day; a table shows which season every month falls in. Each campaign has its own calendar, regions, holidays and settings. The Gregorian presets start with real-world regions of their hemisphere, and the fantasy presets with their world's major regions, each set to a Köppen climate you can change on the Regions tab. The setting presets come with their setting's holidays and moons: the Harptos festival days and Shieldmeet with Selûne, Eberron's Sovereign Host, Silver Flame and Five Nations holidays with its twelve moons, Exandria's holy days with Catha and Ruidus, Golarion's Inner Sea holidays with Somal, and the Galactic Standard Calendar's three festival weeks with thirty well-known worlds as regions (no seasons or moons, since each world has its own). The About & sources page cites each one.
- **Sunrise and sunset:** each season can also set its sunrise and sunset (like 06:30 and 18:15) on its first day, on the new-campaign form and in *Settings*. Days in between blend from one season's times to the next, wrapping into the next year, and the season table shows the times on the first of every month. The day page (and the Current Day tab) shows the day's sunrise, sunset and hours of daylight, and the Markdown export lists them. Regions in the other hemisphere take the times from half a year away. The times feed visibility (see below). The Gregorian presets come with times for about 45° latitude in local solar time; other presets and older campaigns have none until you add them, and with no times nothing changes.
- **Members tab:** add an approved player, or a co-GM, by the email they sign in with. Each member can set their character name for the campaign (a GM can set anyone's); events and notes they add are signed with it, or with their own name if they have none.
- **Time tracking** (per campaign, picked on the new-campaign form and changeable under *Settings*): four shifts a day (Dawn, Midday, Dusk, Overnight; the default, and what older campaigns keep), two shifts (Day and Night), or hours from midnight like a normal calendar. Everything below that says "shift" means an hour in hours mode. Weather, light and visibility are still worked out for the day's four quarters: Day takes Midday's and Night takes Overnight's, and each hour takes the quarter it falls in (the hours before dawn take the overnight one). Switching modes moves the clock, shift activities, shift notes, timed events and rests or downtime to the same time of day; going to fewer, longer steps joins activities that land together. In hours mode the day page folds hours with nothing recorded, and the week view lists only hours with an activity.
- **Pass time** (GM, in the one-line bar at the top of every campaign page: a row on Current Day and the calendar, a *⏩ Pass time* button that opens it everywhere else): move ahead by shifts, days, weeks, months or years. You can also label the passed shifts, e.g. "Coach journey". Weather is generated for each day as time passes, and stays fixed once generated. Each day's weather is its forecast; the four shifts vary within it (cooler overnight, wind easing at night, rain thinning to drizzle, morning fog lifting, afternoon storms), never worse than the forecast except where a colder shift turns rain to snow. Shift weather is worked out from the stored day, so older days get it too. The day page also shows the **ground**: snow lying (with its depth, melting once it warms up), frozen ground, or how wet it is (Dry, Firm, Damp, Soft, Muddy, Waterlogged), for the day and for each shift, with what that means for travel and when it last snowed or rained in its tooltip. It is worked out from the last 60 days of weather in the party's region (snow and rain, temperature, sun and wind), so nothing extra is stored; days with no weather recorded there are rolled from the region's climate. Caves, ships and space have none, and players get it only as far as they can see the weather.
- **Current Day** (the tab a campaign opens on, for GMs and players alike): today up close, with the current shift's weather, visibility and activity large, the day's four shifts at a glance, then the full day page below it. *Previous day* and *Next day* step through the days without leaving the tab, and *Return to today* comes back. The day page is a column of boxes, in this order: **Weather** (the current shift, the day's conditions and region, and the GM's weather editor), **Shifts** (or **Hours**), **Notes**, **Events** and **Reminders**. Click a box's heading to fold it to just that heading (Weather folds to a line with the region and the day's weather); which boxes are folded is remembered in that browser for each account. The **Notes** box holds *Locations visited* and two notes: **Public notes**, which everyone in the campaign reads, and **Secret notes**, which only GMs ever see (the server never sends them to players). The GM writes both in a word-processor editor (headings, bold, italics, underline, strikethrough, numbered and bulleted lists, quotes, links and tables, with undo and redo; Tab moves between table cells); notes written before it show as they were typed until they are next saved.
- **Calendar:** month grid, week view with all four shifts, or year view. Click any day to drill down to weather, moons, holidays, per-shift activity, visibility and events. Both views preview the notes on each day: the month grid shows the first note in two lines (just a 📝 on a phone) with every note in its tooltip, and the week view shows day notes under the date and each shift's notes in its shift. Players get only what they can read: day notes and shift notes, never GM notes. The year view shows every month of the year on one page, a small grid each with today ringed, days with events or holidays in bold (a holiday also underlined in its colour), and the day's date, weather, moon phases, holidays, events, reminders and note count in its tooltip; ◀ ▶ step a year at a time, a month's name opens it in the month grid, and a day opens the day page.
- **Day page** (GM): change the day's region, edit or re-roll the weather, and write the Public and Secret notes. For each shift you can enter activity and private notes, override visibility, or make that shift "now". *Export* downloads the day as a Markdown file (events, weather, notes and shifts) laid out like Sinless's Scabard export, for pasting into Scabard or another wiki; a player's file holds only what that player can see. *Add an action* offers 18 common shift tasks (travel, navigate, scout ahead, keep watch, forage, hunt, make camp, stand guard, sleep and so on); each one adds its name to the activity, which stays free text, and shows what it involves. The pass-time activity box suggests the same actions.
- **Events:** single-day or multi-day, all day or in one shift. GMs can mark an event **Secret** so players never receive it; this is filtered on the server. Players can add notes to any event they can see. Every shift on the day page also has its own notes box, separate from events, where players and GMs add notes.
- **Events tab:** every event in the campaign on one list, oldest or newest first, with its dates, shifts, tags, the start of its details and how many notes it has; click one to open its day. The search box looks through titles, details, event notes, tags and who added it, matches every word you type, and highlights them. Tag buttons narrow the list to events carrying any of the tags picked, and *When* shows all events, only those today or later (an event still running today counts), or only those before today. The filters stay as you left them while you move around the campaign. Players never receive secret events here either. The **List | Timeline** switch at the top (remembered in that browser) draws the same filtered events on a timeline in the campaign's own calendar, time running left to right: one lane per tag (or per Encyclopedia entry the events name, or one lane for all), a dot for a one-day event and a bar for a longer one, and close up an event with shifts covers just those. Zoom with the mouse wheel, a pinch, the Era / Year / Month / Week / Day buttons or + and −; drag or use the arrow keys to move along; *Now* and *Fit* jump to the current day or to every event. Bands for sessions, seasons and holidays switch on and off, a red line marks now, secret events have a dashed 🔒 edge, hovering (or tapping) an event shows it and clicking it (or tapping again) opens its day, and *⬇ PNG* downloads the timeline as a picture for the session recap. A phone starts at month zoom with one lane.
- **Travel tab:** calculates travel time, then *Depart* passes that much time. It labels the transit shifts, logs a travel event, and can change the region during travel and on arrival. Modes:
  - Overland / sea / air: speed × hours per day × terrain.
  - Hohmann orbital transfer: time, Δv, and how often launch windows repeat. Includes the Sol, Jupiter, Saturn and Earth–Moon systems.
  - Constant-thrust flip-and-burn at any g, with an optional speed cap.
  - Relativistic interstellar: shows ship time and outside-world time.
  - FTL: a fixed jump duration, or a speed in light-years per day.
  - Hyperspace (Star Wars): a trip's time for a Class 1 hyperdrive on a main hyperlane (or a sample trip), times the drive's class (Class 0.5 is twice as fast, Class 2 half as fast) and the route (×2 off the main lanes, ×4 uncharted).
- **Downtime tab:** one card per rest or stretch of downtime. *New downtime* (GM) asks for a **Long Rest** (uses up at least half the day: 2 of 4 shifts, 1 of 2, or 8 hours), a **Short Rest** (about an hour; 0 shifts marks the current shift without losing any) or **Downtime** (a start and end date). It writes Long Rest, Short Rest or Downtime into the activity of every shift it covers, after anything already there, and can move the clock to its end. On each card the GM ticks what the party does (rests: Tend to Wounds, Manage Stress, Repair Gear, Prepare, Relax, plus Work on Project on a long rest; downtime: Work on Project, Crafting, Career Pursuits, Research, Recuperate, Train, Networking, Acquire an Asset, Indulge or Carouse), adds custom activities, and puts players on each one. Each card has notes players can read and edit, and GM notes they never receive. Deleting a card takes its label back off those shifts.
- **Reminders:** the day page (and the Current Day tab) has a *Reminders* box. *Add reminder* takes a title, a date and a shift (or hour), and details; it pops up in a window once the campaign clock reaches that shift, on the GM's page when they pass time and on a player's open page at its next check. *Dismiss* stops it popping up for you; *Remind me later* puts it off until the clock moves again or the page is reloaded. Anyone can keep reminders for themselves; a GM can also share one with the GMs or with everyone. Days with a reminder show a 🔔 on the calendar. Moving a reminder to a new time sets it off again for everyone, and switching the time tracking moves it with the clock.
- **Party Holdings tab:** what the party owns, open to the GM and every player alike. On the left, *Party Funds* is a ledger of the party's money, one line per currency with where it is kept and its amount, and below it *Gear and Loot* lists each item with who claimed it and where it is (all free text, saved as you leave each box) and a − / + count. On the right, *Add holding* makes a card for a Vehicle, Animal, Building, Asset or a type you name yourself, with a location, description, count and tags (a list of their own, separate from the events' tags). Each type adds its own fields: a vehicle has Top Speed, Fuel Capacity, Fuel Type, Fuel Consumption, Reliability, Armor, Crew, Cargo and Value; an animal Top Speed, Training, Armor, Crew, Cargo and Value; a building a Construction Type (Wood, Stone, Concrete, Metal, Mixed or your own), Armor, Size, Class, Status, Upkeep, Access, Seclusion, Environment and Value; an asset a Category (Item, Connection, Contact, Favor or your own) and Value; a custom type just Value. Each card also has a *Storage* ledger of what is kept in that holding and what each item weighs; the total is shown under it, and flagged when it is more than the holding's Cargo (the first number in Cargo is compared, so use the same unit for both). Cards have Edit and Delete.
- **Encyclopedia Categories and filters:** every entry can have one Category (Location, Person, Item, Group or Vehicle), picked in its editor, and the list is grouped by Category (each group folds away with a click on its heading), with *No category* last. Above the list, the *Category*, *Tags* and *Connections* dropdowns each tick what to show, with *Select all* and *Deselect all* (and a box to find one when there are many); everything starts ticked, so *Deselect all* then ticking *City* shows only cities. Each option counts the entries ticking it would show, given the other filters, so within *Location* the Tags dropdown says how many are cities or inns. *No tags* and *No connections* cover the entries without any. *Show all* clears the search and every filter. Needs migration 018, which gives every entry tagged Location, Person, Item, Group or Vehicle that Category and takes the tag off (an entry with more than one takes the first of Person, Location, Group, Item, Vehicle and keeps the others as tags). The Proper Noun Detector's guess, the Generators' *📖 Add* and the Scabard import set the Category too.
- **Encyclopedia connections:** on an entry's page, *🔗 Link* opens a dialog to connect it to one or more other entries at once (tick them, filtered by search or tag) with one kind of connection. Each kind has a label for each end, so London shows *Has resident* Gunther and Gunther shows *Resident of* London; a kind with one label (*Ally of*, or one you type like *Residents*) reads the same both ways. Starter kinds are offered, and new ones are made by typing them in the dialog. The *Connections* card lists them by label on both pages, with an optional note, ✎ to add or untick entries and × to remove one (whoever made it, or a GM). *Suggested connections* offers the entries named in the notes, or whose notes name this one, and reads a line like `Residents: Gunther Hoffmann, Mother Ilse` as a suggested label. The Encyclopedia list searches connection labels and connected names, filters by the Connections dropdown, and *Show in list* lists an entry with everything connected to it. GMs can mark a connection GM only, and one that touches a GM-only entry always is: the server never sends either to players, nor a label used only by them. GMs rename, recolour, merge or delete kinds under *Kinds of connection*.
- **Find Image:** in an Encyclopedia entry's *Pictures and files*, *🖼️ Find Image* searches for a picture, starting from the entry's name (change the words to "ruined keep" for a place of your own). *Open web* searches Openverse (Flickr, Wikimedia Commons, museums and more), *Museums* the public-domain works of The Met, the Art Institute of Chicago and (with an api.data.gov key in the config) the Smithsonian, and *DeviantArt* its tags. Each result shows its title, artist, licence and source, all linked. *Use this picture* copies one to the entry with that credit (entry pictures show alone, with a *?* on the corner for the name, caption and credit on hover or tap; the picture at full size shows it with its links); it is offered only for CC0, public domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA pictures. A DeviantArt work under any other licence offers *View on DeviantArt*, *Artist's page* and *Embed from DeviantArt*, which keeps only the link and shows the picture from DeviantArt (refreshed through its oEmbed every 6 hours; if the artist removes it, the entry says so). The server does all the searching, so the keys in the config's `image_search` block stay there; DeviantArt needs an app registered at deviantart.com/developers. Needs migration 017. An entry's pictures sit below its notes and connections, and hovering a linked entry name anywhere shows its first picture with the tooltip.
- **Fan wikis:** the GM picks the campaign's fan wiki in *Settings* (grouped by setting: Wookieepedia, Memory Alpha, Tolkien Gateway, Sarna's BattleTechWiki, the Forgotten Realms Wiki and two dozen more; or None for a game of its own; the list starts on a guess from the campaign's name and calendar, used only once saved). An Encyclopedia entry then has *🗂️ Summary from …*, which lists the wiki's matching articles and adds the picked one's opening paragraphs to the Public notes with a link and the licence the wiki states (asked of the wiki itself). Find Image gets a tab for the wiki: openly licensed pictures are copied with their credit, and the rest (most fan wiki pictures are fair-use screenshots and official art) can be kept as *Personal fair use*, credited to the wiki. The server does every request. The module is self-contained (`api/fanwikis*.php`, `static/fanwikis.js`): `'fan_wikis' => ['open_only' => true]` in the config keeps only openly licensed text and pictures, `'enabled' => false` turns it off, and `tools/fanwiki-kill-switch.sh --non-open` (or `--all`) takes the non-open wikis (or the whole module) out of the code. No database change: the pick is kept in the campaign's settings.
- **Library tab** (just under Encyclopedia): the campaign's books, journals and notes. Every member, GM or player, can add a tome and change any of them: pick Book, Journal or Note, give it a title and a short description, and write it out in the same word-processor editor as the day notes, in a tall box for long texts. Encyclopedia names in a tome are linked to their entries, and saving one runs the Proper Noun Detector over it like any note. *Scan campaign* reads the Library too, and Party Holdings (holdings, Storage, Gear and Loot and Party Funds) and downtime cards; a holding or a downtime card also offers its new names when it is saved, while the ledger lines, which save as you type, are left to the scan. Anyone can also add pictures (maps, handouts, illustrations) and files to a tome, from its page or while editing it, and take them off again; they use the same uploads as Encyclopedia entries, and deleting the tome deletes them. The list down the left searches titles and descriptions and filters by kind. If someone else saves a tome while you have it open, your save is refused with their name rather than overwriting their words, so copy what you wrote, reload and add it again. Whoever added a tome, or a GM, can delete it.
- **Generators tab:** random names, places, businesses, rumors and towns for when the players go somewhere you didn't plan, for everyone in the campaign. Pick a genre at the top (Fantasy, Science fiction, Cyberpunk, Modern day or Wild West; remembered in that browser for each campaign), then *🎲 Generate* on any card: **Names** by race or people (Human, Elf, Dwarf, Halfling, Gnome and Orc in fantasy; spacers, synthetics, insectoids and reptilians in science fiction; street names, netrunner handles, corporate names and rogue AIs in cyberpunk; English-speaking, Spanish-speaking, French, German, Russian, Arabic, Indian, Chinese and Japanese names in the modern day (Chinese and Japanese family name first; these also mix into the spacers and street names); settlers, outlaws and lawmen in the Wild West) and gender (feminine, masculine, gender-neutral or any), each shown as just the name; its *Kind* switches from real names to street names, cyberpunk handles or the tags netrunners sign in cyberspace (or any of them), in every genre, each with the real name behind it made for the chosen people and gender (tick *Add a look and a quirk* for an age, build and a couple of features, and a habit or manner, which 📖 Add keeps as the entry's notes); a batch of names never repeats a name or a family name, **Taverns, inns & pubs** (saloons, bars and clubs in the other genres) with what each is known for and who runs it, **Companies** by line of business, **Rumors** with who the party heard each from, about the people, places, groups and items in the campaign's Encyclopedia (by their Person, Location, Group and Item Categories, or tags of those names; untick *Use Encyclopedia names* for made-up ones only), and a whole **Town** of a chosen size: who runs it, its trade, defences and a notable feature, a few places and people, and two bits of trouble. *📖 Add* puts any result, or any place or person in a town, in the Encyclopedia (in the Person, Location or Group Category, or tagged Rumor, with its write-up as the Public notes); a GM's go in as GM only unless they untick the box. Nothing is stored otherwise. Every word list and template is the app's own (`static/generators.js`), written for it rather than taken from other generators; the real-world names are common names picked by hand, not copied from a name database.
- **Maps tab** (under World, after Generators): a GM adds a map picture (uploaded, or found with *Find Image*) and sets its scale, either by clicking two places a known distance apart or by counting grid squares (a file name like `Keep_30x20.png` or `_70px` fills the square size in, and *Roll20 (70 px = 5 ft)* fills in Roll20's default). Everyone can zoom, pan and *Measure*, in their own units; a map scaled in astronomical units or light-years is a space map and measures in AU and light-years for everyone. GMs save measured paths as routes. Players add public note pins ("Added by" their character), and GMs pin Encyclopedia entries and events, make new ones with *New entry here* and *New event here*, and turn a player's pin into an entry with *Add to Encyclopedia*. An entry or event with a pin links back to it with 📍. A GM-only map, a GM-only pin and a pin on a GM-only entry or secret event never reach players. The party has a marker on the map, a flag or its token, and the Travel tab's *Where the party is* card splits the party into groups and merges them again; *Depart* asks who is travelling and moves that group's marker to the pin named in *To*. The main group's region is the campaign's current region, so the weather follows it; a side group's trip moves the clock but doesn't label shifts or change the weather. The viewer is Leaflet 1.9.4, bundled in `static/vendor/leaflet`. Needs migration 019.
- **At the Table** (first under Party): the campaign right now in big type, for the table, a phone, beside a virtual tabletop or on a stream: the date and shift, holidays, the weather, light and moons, where the party is (its region, and the pin nearest its marker), today's events and reminders, an Encyclopedia lookup, and *Add a note* on the current shift or at the end of an entry's notes (a GM can put an entry note in its GM notes). GMs get *Back a shift*, *Pass a shift* and *+1 day*. **Panel links** put the same view on `panel.html`, which other sites may frame: *Use in a VTT or on stream → New panel link…* makes a link that sees what you see (anyone), or a *Public* one, read-only and showing only what every player sees, with an *Overlay* style for an OBS browser source (GMs). Paste the link into the panel once; it follows the campaign as time passes, and *Revoke* stops it at once. A link signs in with a code in a header (`api/panel.php`), as a browser won't send Chronicle's cookie inside another site's frame, and reaches only its own campaign and a short list of actions. **Owlbear Rodeo:** add `<base_url>/api/index.php?a=owlbear_manifest` under *Extensions → Add custom extension*, and the panel opens from Owlbear's toolbar. Needs migration 022.
- **Tokens tab** (Setup, after Members, GMs only): a library of token pictures. *Upload tokens…* takes many at once, each named after its file, and every token has a kind (Party, Character, Creature, Object), a size in squares and GM only. Assign one to a party group (it becomes the group's marker), a member's character (shown on the Members tab) or an Encyclopedia entry. Deleting a token clears what used it. A GM-only token's picture never reaches players.
- **Session notes and the Sessions tab:** the **Sessions** tab (after Events) lists each game night; *+ Session* starts one. A GM's session page has a notes box for running notes: type freely, a line per note (Enter makes a new line, Tab and Shift+Tab indent a line under the one above, ⇥ ⇤ buttons on a phone, `@` names an Encyclopedia entry) and press *Add to notes* (or Ctrl+Enter) to add them to the session; what is typed stays in the browser until then. A shorthand covers the rest: a line starting `!` is a GM aside (dice, rules) left out of the summary, `?` a loose end, and `>` a time jump (`>+1`, `>+9`, `>+2w`, `>dusk`, `>+1 dawn`, `>12 Firstmonth`, or `>` alone for the clock) that the notes after it follow; its divider has *Set the clock here* to move the campaign's clock too. The box's tooltip and its `?` button list the shorthand. The summary is the page's wide column, with the notes beside it (a phone shows one at a time, the summary first once there is one); under the title, one line holds when it was played, *Published*, *✎ Details* (the title and date played, open until the session has a title; the one *Save* under the summary saves them with everything else), *Export* and a ⋯ menu (End session or Reopen, Include notes, Delete). Notes are grouped by when they happened: once they cover more than one shift, a heading over each run names its day and shift (a `>` jump heads the notes after it), and a note's 🕓 moves that one note in time. Each note is ticked once the summary covers it, with *Build draft* filling the summary from the notes (a paragraph per note with its indented lines, a heading per in-game day, `!` lines left out, `?` lines and 🔒 blocks in GM-only paragraphs) for the GM to rewrite. *Build draft* and *Save* sit beside the Summary heading. Under the summary sit *Session Rewards* and *Loot Found* side by side, written the same way and saved with it; players read them once the session is published and they go in the Markdown export. On Current Day, the Notes box's *Import to a session…* copies the day's Public and/or Secret notes into a session (a new one or any existing one); Secret notes go in under a 🔒 line. *Wide view* gives a session the whole width, with the sessions along the top. Players read a session's summary once it is *Published*, never the notes. *Export* downloads a session as Markdown, the GM's optionally with the notes as a nested list. Summaries are in Search and the Encyclopedia's *Mentioned in*; GMs' searches find the notes too. Under it all, *Pictures and files* keeps a session's maps, handouts and sketches (the same uploads as an Encyclopedia entry's, with a caption and an optional 🔒 GM only): GMs add them, players see them once the session is published, and each picture opens in a viewer that zooms (a click, + and −, 1:1, or a trackpad pinch) and pans by dragging or a finger, so a big map can be read on a phone too. Deleting a session deletes its files. (No database change: the attachments table takes them as `parent_type` `session`.)
- **GM-only paragraphs:** in a day's Public notes, an Encyclopedia entry's public notes and a Library tome, a GM can mark paragraphs GM only with the editor's 🔒 button. They read as a band labelled *GM only*, and the server takes them out of everything a player is sent (the day page and calendar previews, the Encyclopedia, the Library, Mentioned in, Search and the export). When a player saves an entry or a tome, the server keeps the GM's hidden paragraphs where they were. A name found only in one is offered to the Encyclopedia as GM only. On an entry's or a tome's page, *🔓 Reveal* on a GM-only band makes it public in one click, and can log it as a *Revealed* event on the current day.
- **View as player** (GM): the 👁 button beside *GM view* shows the campaign exactly as players see it, answered by the server as it would a player, until *Leave player view*. Nothing can be changed meanwhile.
- **Writing in the rich-text editor:** every notes editor (day notes, summaries, Encyclopedia entries, tomes) keeps its common tools on one line (style, bold, italic, underline, lists, link and 🔒); ⋯ shows the rest (strikethrough, quote, clear formatting, tables, undo and redo). On a desktop the toolbar stays in sight as the text scrolls, and an editor grows with its text instead of scrolling inside a box. ⤢ *Focus* gives one editor the whole window at reading width; Esc or ⤡ brings it back, and the page's own Save saves it. An Encyclopedia entry's editor puts the Public and GM notes straight under the name, with the tags, *Where it was found* (folded, with a count, unless the entry is blank) and *Generate Summary* after them.
- **@ mentions:** in any rich-text note, type `@` and a few letters to pick an Encyclopedia name (or one of its other names) from a list; its name is written in and linked as usual. The last line adds what you typed as a new entry. Escape leaves the `@` as typed.
- **Search:** the box at the top of the Legend (at the end of the tabs on narrower screens, a 🔍 tab on a phone; press `/` to jump to it) searches the whole campaign: Encyclopedia entries by name, alias, tag and notes, events with their tags and notes, day notes and Locations visited, shift activity and notes, reminders, downtime, Party Holdings and the Library. Every word typed has to appear, in any order. Results are grouped by kind, names and titles that match first, each with the words highlighted and a link to its page. A player's search only reaches what the server would show them anyway, so it never finds a secret; a GM's secret results are marked 🔒.
- **Change notices:** a campaign page that is open checks every 30 seconds (and when you come back to the tab) whether a GM has changed the campaign: the calendar, the clock, events, notes, shifts, regions, holidays, downtime, party holdings or members. If so, a banner under the top bar says *The GM updated the calendar* with a *Refresh* button. The page never reloads on its own, so nothing you are typing is lost; save it first, then press Refresh. Your own saves don't raise the banner.
- **Regions:** each region sets a climate, a hemisphere, a light source (sun and moons, artificial, or none) and a terrain modifier to visibility. The *Party is here* button sets the region used for new days.
- **Climates:** pick any of the 30 Köppen–Geiger classes (Af rainforest through EF ice cap), one of the simple setting climates (temperate, desert, underground, starship…), or build your own: choose the Köppen letters, load that class's baseline, then tune each season's range of daytime highs, precipitation (0–5) and how often storms, fog and dust turn up (0–3). The region form shows the resulting baseline weather and odds for each season. The new-campaign form sets the home region's climate. Each class's highs and wetness come from one representative station's 1991–2020 monthly normals (`tools/climate-normals.json`; `node tools/climate-baselines.js` shows the rules and checks each station's class).
- **Weeks:** a calendar's week is as long as its list of weekdays: 7 for Gregorian, Middle-earth and Glorantha, 8 for the Imperial Calendar, 6 for Standard Ship Time, or any number on a custom calendar (none for a calendar without weeks). The month grid has a column per weekday, the week view and *Pass time* by weeks use that length, and Nth weekday holidays go up to as many as fit in the longest month.
- **Eras:** a calendar can name stretches of years, one per line under *Settings*: a name, an abbreviation and the first year, like `Dale Reckoning, DR, 1`. An era lasts until the next one starts, and its abbreviation labels its years (1492 DR) in place of the year suffix. Add `restart` and the era counts its own years from 1 (a king's reign), or `before` and it covers the years before its first year, counted back (1 BCE, 2 BCE; the Gregorian presets have this, so there is no year 0). Years are still stored as calendar years, so adding or changing eras only re-labels them. Date pickers take the year in an era, with the era picked next to it, the month title's tooltip names the era, and `{E}` in the date format shows its full name.
- **Holidays:** same date every year, Nth weekday of a month (counted from the start, or from the end: the last Friday, the 2nd to last Monday), one-time, every N days, or a moon phase (every full moon of a chosen moon, in any month or only one). Same-date and Nth-weekday holidays can repeat every N years instead of every year (every 4 years on Midsummer), counted from a first year. Any repeating holiday can run forever, only between two years, or a set number of times (moon-phase holidays take the years only). Each can last several days and can be secret.
- **About & sources** (footer link, readable signed out too): where every built-in figure comes from: each travel-planner speed and distance, the orbits and star positions, the station behind each Köppen baseline, the calendar presets and holidays, and a plain list of what has no open source (the fictional settings, the game tuning).
- **Settings:** time scale, default units (a temperature unit; °F and °R also show distances, depths and speeds in Imperial, the rest in Metric, for anyone who hasn't picked their own), whether players see future weather (when they don't, they get today's weather one shift at a time as the clock reaches it, and never the day's forecast, which would give the later shifts away; the server holds the rest back), whether players may add events, and a full calendar editor. The editor covers months, festival days that sit outside the week, leap rules, eras, seasons (with the hemisphere their dates are for; regions in the other hemisphere get the opposite season), moons (including erratic ones like Morrslieb), shift names, date format and hours per day.
- **Calendar export and import:** *Settings → Tools → Export calendar* downloads the campaign's calendar as a JSON file: months, weekdays, leap rule, moons, seasons, shift names and date format, with the campaign's holidays and the date it had reached. Days, events, notes and regions are not in it. *Import calendar…* on the same card replaces the calendar with one from a file (stored days keep their absolute day numbers, as with a preset) and offers to add the file's holidays the campaign doesn't have yet. On the new-campaign form, *Import calendar from file…* starts a campaign on an exported calendar, with its holidays and starting on the date it had reached, which is how to copy one campaign's calendar to another. Import also takes a bare calendar object, like the one in Settings' *raw calendar JSON* box.
- **Import from Scabard:** *Settings → Tools → ⬆ Import from Scabard…* brings a campaign over from [Scabard](https://www.scabard.com). Pick the `.json` file its *Export campaign* writes; a dialog says what is in it before anything is written. Characters, places, groups, items, notes and adventures become Encyclopedia entries in the Person, Location, Group and Item Categories, or tagged Note and Adventure, with their other names, the brief summary and description as the Public notes, the GM secrets as the GM notes, secret pages as GM only, and Scabard's categories (City, NPC…) as more tags. Dated events (`2025-10-05: Final Preparations`) become Sessions played on that date, their description as the summary, the secrets as GM-only paragraphs and the participants, setting and the adventure they were part of as lines at the end (or Encyclopedia entries tagged Event, if you choose; undated events always are). Scabard's connections become connections here, read from the right end (*Participant in* / *Has participant*, *Located in* / *Contains*, *Leader of* / *Led by*, a symmetric *Neighbor of*…; a former one is noted, a secret one is GM only), and each page's picture is copied from scabard.com with a credit, if you leave that ticked. Everything is matched by name, so the import can be run again after Scabard changes: an entry that exists is kept (one with nothing written in it yet is filled in), and tags, connections and pictures are added only where missing. Nothing is deleted. Attributes, tabs and comments are left out. (`api/scabard.php`, `static/scabard.js`; no database change.)

## How visibility is scored (0 Pitch black … 5 Bright)

The score starts from a base value for the shift: dawn 3, midday 5, dusk 1, overnight 0. Each day starts at Dawn and ends with the Overnight shift after it. Artificially lit regions use their own level for each shift instead. Then:

- **Sunrise and sunset** (when the seasons set them, natural light only): each shift is placed on the clock (Dawn from a quarter of the day, Midday from half, Dusk from three quarters, Overnight from midnight) and gains or loses light against an even 06:00–18:00 day, so a long summer evening brightens Dusk and a winter morning darkens Dawn; the tooltip shows it as, say, *sun 07:40–16:20 −1*. In hours mode each hour takes the light of the sun instead of its quarter's base: daylight 5, the hour after sunrise and before sunset 4, an hour of twilight either side 2, night 0, with moonlight up to +2 at night and +1 in twilight.
- **Moonlight:** up to +2 overnight (+1 at dusk) depending on how much of the moons is lit, reduced by cloud cover.
- **Weather:** that shift's weather, with a modifier such as fog −3, or a blizzard −4 by day.
- **Terrain:** the region's modifier.
- **GM override:** if set on that shift, it replaces the result.

Hover over any visibility badge to see this breakdown.

## Notes

- Every day is stored by an absolute day number. Editing a calendar re-labels existing days instead of moving their data.
- The Imperial preset's weekday alignment and moon starting phases are arbitrary. Adjust them in Settings if your campaign has canon dates.
- Some setting details are the app's choice where the setting gives none: Eberron's moon cycles, Catha's and Ruidus's cycles and the weekday Exandria's year starts on, Somal's phases on Golarion, and the Galactic Standard Calendar's month names (Month 1 to Month 10) and its hyperdrive and route figures beyond Class 0.5 and 1. Adjust them in Settings if your campaign has canon dates.
- Shared with Sinless: the sign-in flow, the users and rate-limit tables, the server library, error bodies (`{"error": "code", "message": "…"}`), the UI kit in `static/kit.js` and the CSS token names (`--bg --panel --panel-2 --line --text --dim --accent --on-accent --ok --bad --disp --body --mono`).
- Security: every query uses prepared statements, every change needs a CSRF token, and the session cookie is HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax with idle and absolute timeouts. Sign-in is rate-limited per IP in the database. `.htaccess` forces HTTPS and sends HSTS, CSP and framing headers, and blocks the config, `db/`, `tests/` and `.git`. User text is never inserted as HTML, with one guarded exception: the day's Public and Secret notes are rich text, stored as HTML that `api/richtext.php` cleans to a strict allow-list on every save (paragraphs, headings, bold, italics, underline, strikethrough, code, lists, quotes, tables, and links to http, https or mailto only; no scripts, styles, attributes, images or event handlers), and that `static/richtext.js` rebuilds element by element from the same list before showing it, never through innerHTML.
