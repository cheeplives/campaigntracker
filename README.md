# Campaign Chronicle — RPG campaign tracker

A web-based tracker for tabletop campaigns: in-game calendar kept in four shifts, two shifts (Day and Night) or hours a day, weather by
region and season, moon phases, visibility, holidays, events with secret GM entries, player notes,
and a travel planner for overland, orbital and interstellar trips.

PHP 8 + MySQL on the server, plain JavaScript in the browser. No build step or frameworks.

Players sign in with **Google or GitHub**, the same way as the Sinless app on the same domain, and new accounts wait for an admin to approve them.

## Install

The full runbook (OAuth apps, config fields, upgrading an older install) is in [docs/DEPLOY.md](docs/DEPLOY.md). In short:

1. **Create a MySQL database** in your host's panel, or reuse the Sinless database so the two apps share accounts and one approval queue (fresh installs only).
2. **Deploy with git:** `git clone https://github.com/cheeplives/campaigntracker.git chronicle` inside your web root. Updates are `./deploy.sh` (pull, PHP syntax check, database upgrade).
3. **Configure:** copy `api/config.example.php` to `~/chronicle-config.php` (above the web root; `api/config.php` also works) and fill in the database, `base_url`, the Google/GitHub client ids and secrets, and your email under `admin_identities`.
4. **Add the callback** `<base_url>/api/auth/callback.php` to your Google OAuth client (as a second redirect URI next to Sinless's) and register a GitHub OAuth app for it.
5. **Install:** run `php tools/migrate.php` in the app folder to create the tables. (Or set `'allow_install' => true`, open `<base_url>/install.php` and press *Install or upgrade*, then set it back to `false`.) Later upgrades happen by themselves: `./deploy.sh` runs `tools/migrate.php` after each pull, backing up the database first.
6. **Sign in** with the email in `admin_identities`. You're approved as admin straight away; everyone else appears under *Users* to approve.

## Using it

- **Install as an app** (Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers): the install icon at the right of the address bar, or *Install Campaign Chronicle* in the browser's menu, adds it to the Start menu, dock or home screen and opens it in its own window. On iPhone and iPad, Safari's *Share → Add to Home Screen* does the same. It's still the website, signed in the same way and always up to date; with no connection it shows a short offline notice.
- **Users** (admins only): approve or revoke people after their first sign-in. Accounts from before Google/GitHub sign-in show as *not linked yet*; enter the player's email and their first sign-in with it takes over the old account and its campaigns.
- **Finding your way:** a campaign's tabs are the *Legend* down the left, in four groups (Play, Party, World, Setup) that fold, with « to tuck it to a thin rail. On an iPad held upright the Legend is a drawer: press » on the rail and it slides out over the page, and it slides back once you pick a tab, tap outside it or press Escape. A phone keeps one row of tabs that scrolls sideways. Rarer actions on a row or card (Copy and Delete on the campaign list; Delete on regions, holidays and holdings) wait behind its ⋯ menu, the way a map's setup waits behind its ⚙. Encyclopedia, Library and Sessions are laid out alike: the list on the left with its title, *+ New*, a ⋯ menu (on the Encyclopedia, *Scan campaign* and *Export…*) and, on the Encyclopedia and Library, *Wide view*, then search and filters; the item picked on the right. The « at the list's top right tucks the list to a thin rail so the item picked takes the screen (press the rail to bring it back); each tab remembers it in this browser. On one column (an iPad upright, a phone) there's nothing to tuck. With nothing picked, the right side offers a few to start from (the newest entries, the tomes last changed, the open session and the latest). On a wide screen the list stays beside a long entry and scrolls by itself.
- **Your account** (your name ▾ in the top bar, which also holds *Users* for admins and *Sign out*): *Units* is one choice for everything the app converts: temperatures, travel distances and speeds, snow depth, rainfall and wind speed. *Imperial* shows °F, miles, inches and mph; *Metric* °C, km, cm and km/h; *None* shows each campaign in the units its GM picked (a campaign in °F or °R reads as Imperial, any other as Metric). It only changes what you see, and a campaign set to hide temperatures keeps them hidden. Below it, *Your campaigns* lists every campaign you belong to with your role (GM or Player) and a box for your character's name in each, the same name the Members tab shows; it saves when you leave the box.
- **New campaign:** pick a preset (Gregorian for the Northern or Southern Hemisphere, Imperial Calendar, Middle-earth, Glorantha, Forgotten Realms (Calendar of Harptos), Eberron (Galifar Calendar), Exandria, Golarion (Absalom Reckoning), Star Wars (Galactic Standard Calendar), Starship clock, or a blank custom calendar, which also takes its months and a week of any length with its own day names), a starting date, and the seasons. Each season has a name, a climate (spring, summer, autumn, winter or none) and a first month and day; a table shows which season every month falls in. Each campaign has its own calendar, regions, holidays and settings. The Gregorian presets start with real-world regions of their hemisphere, and the fantasy presets with their world's major regions, each set to a Köppen climate you can change on the Regions tab. The setting presets come with their setting's holidays and moons: the Harptos festival days and Shieldmeet with Selûne, Eberron's Sovereign Host, Silver Flame and Five Nations holidays with its twelve moons, Exandria's holy days with Catha and Ruidus, Golarion's Inner Sea holidays with Somal, and the Galactic Standard Calendar's three festival weeks with thirty well-known worlds as regions (no seasons or moons, since each world has its own). The About & sources page cites each one.
- **Sunrise and sunset:** each season can also set its sunrise and sunset (like 06:30 and 18:15) on its first day, on the new-campaign form and in *Settings*. Days in between blend from one season's times to the next, wrapping into the next year, and the season table shows the times on the first of every month. The day page (and the Current Day tab) shows the day's sunrise, sunset and hours of daylight, and the Markdown export lists them. Regions in the other hemisphere take the times from half a year away. The times feed visibility (see below). The Gregorian presets come with times for about 45° latitude in local solar time; other presets and older campaigns have none until you add them, and with no times nothing changes.
- **Members tab:** add an approved player, or a co-GM, by the email they sign in with. Each member can set their character name for the campaign (a GM can set anyone's); events and notes they add are signed with it, or with their own name if they have none.
- **Time tracking** (per campaign, picked on the new-campaign form and changeable under *Settings*): four shifts a day (Dawn, Midday, Dusk, Overnight; the default, and what older campaigns keep), two shifts (Day and Night), or hours from midnight like a normal calendar. Everything below that says "shift" means an hour in hours mode. Weather, light and visibility are still worked out for the day's four quarters: Day takes Midday's and Night takes Overnight's, and each hour takes the quarter it falls in (the hours before dawn take the overnight one). Switching modes moves the clock, shift activities, shift notes, timed events and rests or downtime to the same time of day; going to fewer, longer steps joins activities that land together. In hours mode the day page folds hours with nothing recorded, and the week view lists only hours with an activity.
- **Pass time** (GM, in the one-line bar at the top of every campaign page: a row on the calendar, a *⏩ Pass time* button that opens it everywhere else): move ahead by shifts, days, weeks, months or years. You can also label the passed shifts, e.g. "Coach journey". Weather is generated for each day as time passes, and stays fixed once generated. Each day's weather is its forecast; the four shifts vary within it (cooler overnight, wind easing at night, rain thinning to drizzle, morning fog lifting, afternoon storms), never worse than the forecast except where a colder shift turns rain to snow. Shift weather is worked out from the stored day, so older days get it too. The day page also shows the **ground**: snow lying (with its depth, melting once it warms up), frozen ground, or how wet it is (Dry, Firm, Damp, Soft, Muddy, Waterlogged), for the day and for each shift, with what that means for travel and when it last snowed or rained in its tooltip. It is worked out from the last 60 days of weather in the party's region (snow and rain, temperature, sun and wind), so nothing extra is stored; days with no weather recorded there are rolled from the region's climate. Caves, ships and space have none, and players get it only as far as they can see the weather.
- **Day pages:** a day of the calendar opens its page. *Previous day* and *Next day* step through the days, and *Go to today* opens At the Table. Old links to the Current Day tab open At the Table, or the day's page for another day. The day page is a column of boxes, in this order: **Weather** (the current shift, the day's conditions, region and *Locations visited*, and the GM's editor, where *Locations visited* sits under the region dropdown and saves with *Save day*; under it, *Places this day* offers the Encyclopedia's Locations named, by name or other name as whole words, in the day's shift activities, players' notes and GM notes, and the entries pinned nearest where the main group's trail had it on the day's shifts, that the box doesn't list yet, and a tap adds one), **Shifts** (or **Hours**), **Notes**, **Events** and **Reminders**. At the Table leaves out the **Notes** box to keep to the day as it runs; a day's notes are on its page in the Calendar. Click a box's heading to fold it to just that heading (Weather folds to a line with the region and the day's weather); which boxes are folded is remembered in that browser for each account. The **Notes** box holds two notes: **Public notes**, which everyone in the campaign reads, and **Secret notes**, which only GMs ever see (the server never sends them to players). The GM writes both in a word-processor editor (headings, bold, italics, underline, strikethrough, numbered and bulleted lists, quotes, links and tables, with undo and redo; Tab moves between table cells); notes written before it show as they were typed until they are next saved.
- **Calendar:** month grid, week view with all four shifts, or year view. Click any day to drill down to weather, moons, holidays, per-shift activity, visibility and events. Both views preview the notes on each day: the month grid shows the first note in two lines (just a 📝 on a phone) with every note in its tooltip, and the week view shows day notes under the date and each shift's notes in its shift. Players get only what they can read: day notes and shift notes, never GM notes. The year view shows every month of the year on one page, a small grid each with today ringed, days with events or holidays in bold (a holiday also underlined in its colour), and the day's date, weather, moon phases, holidays, events, reminders and note count in its tooltip; ◀ ▶ step a year at a time, a month's name opens it in the month grid, and a day opens the day page.
- **Day page** (GM): change the day's region, edit or re-roll the weather, and write the Public and Secret notes. For each shift you can enter activity and private notes, override visibility, or make that shift "now". Each shift shows a summary of what's recorded; press it (or *✎ Edit*) to open its editor, which saves each box as you leave it and says *Saved ✓*; the shift that's now starts open. The Feature Highlighter asks about new names once you leave a shift's editor. *Export* downloads the day as a Markdown file (events, weather, notes and shifts) laid out like Sinless's Scabard export, for pasting into Scabard or another wiki; a player's file holds only what that player can see. The activity is free text. Each shift has **Actions**: one for the party as a whole and one for each player's character, picked from 18 common shift tasks (travel, navigate, scout ahead, keep watch, forage, hunt, make camp, stand guard, sleep and so on), with what each involves shown on hover. A GM picks any of them (in a shift's editor here, or At the Table); a player picks their own At the Table. An "On the move" party action tags the events running then as Transit. A session's page lists the actions of every shift it ran over, and *Build draft* writes them into the summary. Needs migration 032. The pass-time activity box suggests the same actions.
- **Events:** single-day or multi-day, all day or in one shift. GMs can mark an event **Secret** so players never receive it; this is filtered on the server. Players can add notes to any event they can see. Every shift on the day page also has its own notes, separate from events, where players and GMs add notes: *+ Note* opens the box.
- **Events tab:** every event in the campaign on one list, oldest or newest first, with its dates, shifts, tags, the start of its details and how many notes it has; click one to open its day. The search box looks through titles, details, event notes, tags and who added it, matches every word you type, and highlights them. Tag buttons narrow the list to events carrying any of the tags picked, and *When* shows all events, only those today or later (an event still running today counts), or only those before today. The filters stay as you left them while you move around the campaign. Players never receive secret events here either. The **List | Timeline** switch at the top (remembered in that browser) draws the same filtered events on a timeline in the campaign's own calendar, time running left to right: one lane per tag (or per Encyclopedia entry the events name, or one lane for all), a dot for a one-day event and a bar for a longer one, and close up an event with shifts covers just those. Zoom with the mouse wheel, a pinch, the Era / Year / Month / Week / Day buttons or + and −; drag or use the arrow keys to move along; *Now* and *Fit* jump to the current day or to every event. Bands for sessions, seasons and holidays switch on and off, a red line marks now, secret events have a dashed 🔒 edge, hovering (or tapping) an event shows it and clicking it (or tapping again) opens its day, and *⬇ PNG* downloads the timeline as a picture for the session recap. A phone starts at month zoom with one lane.
- **Reminders (on the Events tab):** the **Events | Reminders** tabs along the top of the Events tab's card switch to every reminder you can see on one list (your own, the GMs' if you are a GM, and everyone's), soonest or latest first, each with its date, shift, who it is for, who added it, and whether it is still waiting, due, or dismissed by you. Search looks through titles, details and who added it; *Which reminders* narrows the list to those still to come, due and not dismissed, or dismissed, and a GM can also narrow it by who sees it. *+ New reminder* adds one on any day, and each reminder you may change can be edited or deleted where it is. The address is `#/c/<id>/events/reminders`.
- **Travel tab:** calculates travel time, then *Depart* passes that much time. It labels the transit shifts, logs a travel event, and can change the region during travel and on arrival. Modes:
  - Overland / sea / air: speed × hours per day × terrain.
  - Hohmann orbital transfer: time, Δv, and how often launch windows repeat. Includes the Sol, Jupiter, Saturn and Earth–Moon systems.
  - Constant-thrust flip-and-burn at any g, with an optional speed cap.
  - Relativistic interstellar: shows ship time and outside-world time.
  - FTL: a fixed jump duration, or a speed in light-years per day.
  - Hyperspace (Star Wars): a trip's time for a Class 1 hyperdrive on a main hyperlane (or a sample trip), times the drive's class (Class 0.5 is twice as fast, Class 2 half as fast) and the route (×2 off the main lanes, ×4 uncharted).
- **Downtime tab:** one card per rest or stretch of downtime. *New downtime* (GM) asks for a **Long Rest** (uses up at least half the day: 2 of 4 shifts, 1 of 2, or 8 hours), a **Short Rest** (about an hour; 0 shifts marks the current shift without losing any) or **Downtime** (a start and end date). It writes Long Rest, Short Rest or Downtime into the activity of every shift it covers, after anything already there, and can move the clock to its end. On each card the GM ticks what the party does (rests: Tend to Wounds, Manage Stress, Repair Gear, Prepare, Relax, plus Work on Project on a long rest; downtime: Work on Project, Crafting, Career Pursuits, Research, Recuperate, Train, Networking, Acquire an Asset, Indulge or Carouse), adds custom activities, and puts players on each one. Each card has notes players can read and edit, and GM notes they never receive. Deleting a card takes its label back off those shifts.
- **Reminders:** the day page (and At the Table) has a *Reminders* box. *Add reminder* takes a title, a date and a shift (or hour), and details; it pops up in a window once the campaign clock reaches that shift, on the GM's page when they pass time and on a player's open page at its next check. *Dismiss* stops it popping up for you; *Remind me later* puts it off until the clock moves again or the page is reloaded. Anyone can keep reminders for themselves; a GM can also share one with the GMs or with everyone. Days with a reminder show a 🔔 on the calendar. Moving a reminder to a new time sets it off again for everyone, and switching the time tracking moves it with the clock.
- **Party Holdings tab:** what the party owns, open to the GM and every player alike. On the left, *Party Funds* is a ledger of the party's money, one line per currency with where it is kept and its amount, and below it *Gear and Loot* lists each item with who claimed it and where it is (all free text, saved as you leave each box) and a − / + count. On the right, *+ New holding* makes a card for a Vehicle, Animal, Building, Asset or a type you name yourself, with a location, description, count and tags (a list of their own, separate from the events' tags). Each type adds its own fields: a vehicle has Top Speed, Fuel Capacity, Fuel Type, Fuel Consumption, Reliability, Armor, Crew, Cargo and Value; an animal Top Speed, Training, Armor, Crew, Cargo and Value; a building a Construction Type (Wood, Stone, Concrete, Metal, Mixed or your own), Armor, Size, Class, Status, Upkeep, Access, Seclusion, Environment and Value; an asset a Category (Item, Connection, Contact, Favor or your own) and Value; a custom type just Value. Each card also has a *Storage* ledger of what is kept in that holding and what each item weighs; the total is shown under it, and flagged when it is more than the holding's Cargo (the first number in Cargo is compared, so use the same unit for both). Cards have Edit and Delete.
- **Encyclopedia Categories and filters:** every entry can have one Category (Location, Person, Item, Group or Vehicle), picked in its editor, and the list is grouped by Category (each group folds away with a click on its heading), with *No category* last. Above the list, the *Category*, *Tags* and *Connections* dropdowns each tick what to show, with *Select all* and *Deselect all* (and a box to find one when there are many); everything starts ticked, so *Deselect all* then ticking *City* shows only cities. Each option counts the entries ticking it would show, given the other filters, so within *Location* the Tags dropdown says how many are cities or inns. *No tags* and *No connections* cover the entries without any. *Show all* clears the search and every filter. Needs migration 018, which gives every entry tagged Location, Person, Item, Group or Vehicle that Category and takes the tag off (an entry with more than one takes the first of Person, Location, Group, Item, Vehicle and keeps the others as tags). The Feature Highlighter's guess, the Generators' *📖 Add* and the Scabard import set the Category too.
- **Encyclopedia connections:** on an entry's page, *🔗 Link* opens a dialog to connect it to one or more other entries at once (tick them, filtered by search or tag) with one kind of connection. Each kind has a label for each end, so London shows *Has resident* Gunther and Gunther shows *Resident of* London; a kind with one label (*Ally of*, or one you type like *Residents*) reads the same both ways. Starter kinds are offered, and new ones are made by typing them in the dialog. The *Connections* card lists them by label on both pages, with an optional note, ✎ to add or untick entries and × to remove one (whoever made it, or a GM). *Suggested connections* offers the entries named in the notes, or whose notes name this one, and reads a line like `Residents: Gunther Hoffmann, Mother Ilse` as a suggested label. The Encyclopedia list searches connection labels and connected names, filters by the Connections dropdown, and *Show in list* lists an entry with everything connected to it. GMs can mark a connection GM only, and one that touches a GM-only entry always is: the server never sends either to players, nor a label used only by them. GMs rename, recolour, merge or delete kinds under *Kinds of connection*.
- **Connection web and family tree:** on an entry's page, *🕸 Web* draws it and everything it is connected to as a web, one step out or two (*1 step* and *2 steps*), with each kind of connection in its own colour, an arrow on the ones that read one way, and dashed lines for GM-only connections. The kinds along the top are switches: press one to hide it, and any entry reached only through it goes too. Click an entry to dim the rest and list its connections beside the picture, then *Centre here* to move the web onto it or *Open page* to go there; drag to move the picture and scroll, pinch or +/− to zoom. *🌳 Family* (shown when the entry has a parent, child, partner or sibling connection) lays the family out in generations, parents above children, partners side by side and children under their parents; *Parent of / Child of*, *Partner of* and *Sibling of* are offered with the other kinds, and labels like Mother of, Married to or Brother of count too. The Encyclopedia list's ⋯ menu has *🕸 Campaign web* for every connection at once (the 250 most connected entries when there are more). A player's web holds only the connections the server already shows them. No database change: it is drawn from the connections the page already has (`static/web.js` for the arithmetic, `static/web-view.js` for the dialog).
- **Edit history (GMs):** every save of an Encyclopedia entry, a Library tome, a session's summary, rewards, loot and notes, and a day's notes is kept as a version, with who saved it and when (saves by one person within ten minutes count as one; the newest 50 per item are kept). The *🕘 History* button on each of those opens a list of versions with, on the right, what that save changed word by word (green added, red removed) or how the version differs from now, and *Restore this version*. A restore is itself a new version, so nothing is lost by restoring. Something written before history began shows as *Before history began* once it is next changed. Only GMs see it, as old versions can hold GM notes and secret paragraphs. Database change: migration `036_content_versions.sql` (table `content_versions`); `./deploy.sh` or `php tools/migrate.php` runs it (`static/diff.js` for the arithmetic, `static/history-view.js` for the dialog, `api/history.php`).
- **Sheet templates** (*Sheet templates* in the top bar, readable by every signed-in user): the site's library of character sheet layouts, A to Z by name and searchable by name and game, each showing how many characters use it and how many forks it has. Editions of one game by the same author share a card (under the game's name) with an *Edition* dropdown, which opens on the most used edition; *Use*, *Fork* and *Edit* act on the edition picked. A template with no edition keeps its own card. *Fork* copies anyone's template for you to change; only its author or an admin can edit the original, and nothing in the library is hidden. *New template* opens the editor: sections of fields (text, long text, number, counter, pips, checkbox, choice, list), dragged by their handle to reorder (by mouse or finger), with a live preview, a tick for the fields that belong on the Play view or in the character's summary, and a box size for each (*Small* is half the usual width, *Large* twice it, *Full width* the whole line, or *Custom*, any number of half-columns from 1 to 12; long text and lists run the full width). Each section has a width: *Full width*, or *Half*, *Third* or *Two thirds* to sit beside the sections next to it on a wide screen (on a phone every section takes the full width), and *Compact rows* puts each label beside its box, for long lists like skills. A long label wraps inside a small box, and a list with many columns scrolls sideways inside its box on a phone. A section can name a *Page*: the sheet then shows a tab for each page (sections with no page are on the first, *Sheet*), and the tab stays open while the sheet is in use. Every choice field also offers *Custom…*, which opens a box to type an entry its options don't have. *Import from PDF* drafts a template from a fillable sheet's form fields, or a flat sheet's text, read in your browser: the PDF is never uploaded or stored and is gone when you close the editor. A field can also carry an optional VTT mapping (for example Foundry's `system.attributes.hp.value`), kept in the template for a later import or sync. Site admins load the starter library of about 140 sheets, including older editions of many games, with *Load starter templates* on the *Users* page (again after an update, to refresh them); About lists the sheet each was built from.
- **Characters tab** (first under Party): the campaign's characters, each on a template, as a PC played by a member or an NPC; either can switch to the other. Every character has a Person entry in the Encyclopedia with an *Open sheet* button (and a sheet icon in the list), tagged *PC* or *NPC* (the tag follows the character when it switches); the entry's name is the character's name. A character's *Player* is one of the campaign's players, a GM, or *No player (the GM runs it)*, picked when a GM makes it and changed on its sheet. A character no player of the campaign plays (a hireling, a companion NPC, a PC the GM runs for an absent friend) can *Travel with the party*: it is then listed in its group on the Travel tab's *Where the party is* card and on the map, goes with whoever it is split off with, and joins the group it is merged into. A new PC with no player starts in the party; an NPC joins when the GM ticks it. Needs migration 033. A player's character is in the party with its player; a GM can untick *Travels with the party* on its sheet to take it out (it died, left, or sits this arc out), which takes its player out of every party group while it is their active character, and tick it again to put them back in the main group. Needs migration 034. A GM can also add a sheet to an existing Person entry, with *Add character sheet* on the entry's page or in its editor (where a blank entry opens), shown while its Category is Person or none; once it has one, both offer *Open sheet*. Beside it a GM has *Link a member…*, to say which member plays the person: the character becomes theirs and their active one (for an entry with no sheet, the template picker starts on *None (no sheet yet)*, which only names their character after the entry; pick a template to make its sheet now). The editor has everything the entry's page has: *Copy as handout* (the entry as last saved), *🔍 Scan*, its character card and its Connections, beside Delete, its pins and its pictures. Sheets are open to the party by default; a player can hide any section or field of their own sheet, and a GM's NPC sheets are hidden from players until the GM shows them. A sheet saves each value as you type; when someone else has it open, a line says who is looking at or editing it, and a GM presses *Edit* before changing a player's sheet. *Play* shows just the fields ticked for the table, with big buttons for counters and pips. The sheet's player or a GM can *Delete character*; the Encyclopedia entry stays. *Import* fills a sheet from Pathbuilder 2e (its Export JSON link, file or text) or D&D Beyond (a Public character's link, or its JSON), onto the fields of the starter Pathfinder 2e or D&D 5e (2014 or 2024) template and forks that keep them; it asks first, replaces only the fields it fills and keeps the sheet's name. The server fetches the link, from those two sites only. A player's active character in each campaign (picked on *Your account*) is the character name the rest of the app shows; a player's first PC, or a sheet a GM hands to a player who has none, becomes it. On the *Members* tab a member's character with a sheet links to it, and one named there the old way, with no sheet, offers *Link a character…* (their own sheets and, for a GM, any no other player plays and any Person entry with no sheet yet, the one with the same name first; the character becomes theirs and names them, and an entry gets a sheet on the template the GM picks, or with *None (no sheet yet)*, the first choice, just names them, until *Make a sheet* puts one on it) and *Make a sheet*, which starts a new character with that name and player and, when an Encyclopedia entry already has the name, puts the sheet on that entry.
- **Find Image:** in an Encyclopedia entry's *Pictures and files*, *🖼️ Find Image* searches for a picture, starting from the entry's name (change the words to "ruined keep" for a place of your own). *Open web* searches Openverse (Flickr, Wikimedia Commons, museums and more), *Museums* the public-domain works of The Met, the Art Institute of Chicago and (with an api.data.gov key in the config) the Smithsonian, and *DeviantArt* its tags. Each result shows its title, artist, licence and source, all linked. *Use this picture* copies one to the entry with that credit (entry pictures show alone, with a *?* on the corner for the name, caption and credit on hover or tap; the picture at full size shows it with its links); it is offered only for CC0, public domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA pictures. A DeviantArt work under any other licence offers *View on DeviantArt*, *Artist's page* and *Embed from DeviantArt*, which keeps only the link and shows the picture from DeviantArt (refreshed through its oEmbed every 6 hours; if the artist removes it, the entry says so). The server does all the searching, so the keys in the config's `image_search` block stay there; DeviantArt needs an app registered at deviantart.com/developers. Needs migration 017. An entry's pictures sit below its notes and connections, and hovering a linked entry name anywhere shows its first picture with the tooltip.
- **Fan wikis:** the GM picks the campaign's fan wiki in *Settings* (grouped by setting: Wookieepedia, Memory Alpha, Tolkien Gateway, Sarna's BattleTechWiki, the Forgotten Realms Wiki and two dozen more; or None for a game of its own; the list starts on a guess from the campaign's name and calendar, used only once saved). An Encyclopedia entry then has *🗂️ Summary from …*, which lists the wiki's matching articles and adds the picked one's opening paragraphs to the Public notes with a link and the licence the wiki states (asked of the wiki itself). Find Image gets a tab for the wiki: openly licensed pictures are copied with their credit, and the rest (most fan wiki pictures are fair-use screenshots and official art) can be kept as *Personal fair use*, credited to the wiki. ## How visibility is scored (0 Pitch black … 5 Bright)

The score starts from a base value for the shift: dawn 3, midday 5, dusk 1, overnight 0. Each day starts at Dawn and ends with the Overnight shift after it. Artificially lit regions use their own level for each shift instead. Then:

- **Sunrise and sunset** (when the seasons set them, natural light only): each shift is placed on the clock (Dawn from a quarter of the day, Midday from half, Dusk from three quarters, Overnight from midnight) and gains or loses light against an even 06:00–18:00 day, so a long summer evening brightens Dusk and a winter morning darkens Dawn; the tooltip shows it as, say, *sun 07:40–16:20 −1*. In hours mode each hour takes the light of the sun instead of its quarter's base: daylight 5, the hour after sunrise and before sunset 4, an hour of twilight either side 2, night 0, with moonlight up to +2 at night and +1 in twilight.
- **Moonlight:** up to +2 overnight (+1 at dusk) depending on how much of the moons is lit, reduced by cloud cover.
- **Weather:** that shift's weather, with a modifier such as fog −3, or a blizzard −4 by day.
- **Terrain:** the region's modifier.
- **GM override:** if set on that shift, it replaces the result.

Hover over any visibility badge to see this breakdown.

## Sheet templates from PDFs, with Claude Code

`.claude/skills/sheet-from-pdf/` is a Claude Code skill that turns a character sheet PDF into a starter template in `db/starter-templates/`, laid out like the PDF: box widths from the PDF's boxes, sections side by side where the page has columns, compact rows for skill lists, and a tab for each page. Run Claude Code in the repo and ask it to make a template from a PDF. It uses three local scripts in `tools/sheet-agent/` (they need `npm ci` in `tests/` once):

- `node tools/sheet-agent/extract.js <sheet.pdf> <out-dir>` reads the PDF with the app's own pdf.js: each page's form fields and text with their positions, which fields share a row, where the page splits into columns, likely headings, the width each box would be on the sheet, and the editor's first draft; and a picture of each page.
- `node tools/sheet-agent/preview.js <template.json> <out-dir>` draws a template with the app's own CSS and code, a picture of each page at desktop and iPad widths (add `--widths 1280,820,390` for a phone), and lists what looks wrong (a sheet wider than the screen, a label cut short).
- `node tools/sheet-agent/check.js <template.json>` checks a starter file against the starter test's rules.

The PDF and the work files stay on your computer; only the finished template is committed. Nothing here is deployed.

## Notes

- Every day is stored by an absolute day number. Editing a calendar re-labels existing days instead of moving their data.
- The Imperial preset's weekday alignment and moon starting phases are arbitrary. Adjust them in Settings if your campaign has canon dates.
- Some setting details are the app's choice where the setting gives none: Eberron's moon cycles, Catha's and Ruidus's cycles and the weekday Exandria's year starts on, Somal's phases on Golarion, and the Galactic Standard Calendar's month names (Month 1 to Month 10) and its hyperdrive and route figures beyond Class 0.5 and 1. Adjust them in Settings if your campaign has canon dates.
- Shared with Sinless: the sign-in flow, the users and rate-limit tables, the server library, error bodies (`{"error": "code", "message": "…"}`), the UI kit in `static/kit.js` and the CSS token names (`--bg --panel --panel-2 --line --text --dim --accent --on-accent --ok --bad --disp --body --mono`).
- Security: every query uses prepared statements, every change needs a CSRF token, and the session cookie is HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax with idle and absolute timeouts. Sign-in is rate-limited per IP in the database. `.htaccess` forces HTTPS and sends HSTS, CSP and framing headers, and blocks the config, `db/`, `tests/` and `.git`. User text is never inserted as HTML, with one guarded exception: the day's Public and Secret notes are rich text, stored as HTML that `api/richtext.php` cleans to a strict allow-list on every save (paragraphs, headings, bold, italics, underline, strikethrough, code, lists, quotes, tables, and links to http, https or mailto only; no scripts, styles, attributes, images or event handlers), and that `static/richtext.js` rebuilds element by element from the same list before showing it, never through innerHTML.
