GM's Almanac

Privacy policy

Last updated 27 November 2026

GM's Almanac (gms-almanac.com) is a tracker for tabletop roleplaying campaigns: an in-game calendar, weather, notes, an encyclopedia, maps and character sheets that a game master shares with their players. It is run by one person as a hobby project. This page says what it collects, why, who else sees it, how long it is kept, and how to have it deleted. In short: it keeps what you need to play, it doesn't track you, it shows no ads, and it never sells or rents your information to anyone.

Questions or requests: privacy@gms-almanac.com.

What we collect

When you sign in

You sign in with Google or GitHub. From them we receive, and keep:

  • your email address, so a game master can invite you and so we can tell you when your account is approved;
  • your name as it appears on that account, shown to the people you play with (you can set another name, and pronouns, on your Account page);
  • the provider's account id for you, which is how we recognise you next time;
  • the address of your profile picture, which is stored but not shown anywhere.

We ask Google and GitHub only for this basic profile (Google's openid, email and profile; GitHub's read:user and user:email). We get no access to your email, files, contacts, calendar, repositories or anything else in those accounts, and we never see your password. We also record when you signed up, were approved and last signed in.

What you put in

Everything you and the people in your campaigns write or upload: campaigns, calendar days and their notes, events, sessions, encyclopedia entries, maps, character sheets, clocks, pictures and files, along with who wrote each thing and when (earlier versions of some pages are kept so a game master can look back). Your settings, such as units and theme, are kept with your account.

Automatically

  • A sign-in cookie (chronicle_sid) that keeps you signed in. It is the only cookie we set. There are no advertising or analytics cookies, no tracking pixels, and no third-party scripts.
  • Your browser's own storage for small conveniences on that device, such as the colour theme or a folded sidebar. It stays on your device.
  • Your IP address, held for a few minutes in a counter that limits repeated sign-in attempts, and in the web server's ordinary access and error logs, which our host keeps for a short time.
  • If you use Report a problem, what you send: your message, the page you were on, your browser and screen size, recent errors on the page and, unless you untick it, a screenshot of the page.

How we use it

Only to run GM's Almanac for you: to sign you in, to show each campaign to the people its game master has let in (and keep game-master-only notes from players), to send the emails described below, to keep the site safe from abuse, and to fix problems you report. We don't use your information for advertising, profiling or training AI models, and we don't sell, rent or trade it.

Who else sees it

  • The people in your campaigns see your name (and pronouns, if you set them) and what you write there, as the campaign's settings allow. A game master can see who is in their campaign and invite people by email.
  • The site administrator can see the list of accounts (names and emails) to approve new ones, and reads problem reports. When someone new asks for access, their name and email may be posted to the administrator's own Discord or Slack channel so they can approve it.
  • Our host, DreamHost, runs the server and database the site lives on, and stores the data on our behalf.
  • Google or GitHub handle the sign-in itself, under their own privacy policies.
  • Email: approval notices and campaign invitations are sent through our host's mail service.
  • Features you choose to use talk to outside services, and only then:
    • Find Image sends the words you search for (never your name or email) from our server to the picture library you pick (Openverse, Wikimedia Commons, museum collections such as The Met and the Art Institute of Chicago, Pixabay, Pexels, Europeana, the Smithsonian or DeviantArt), and your browser then loads the pictures from them. The About & sources page lists every source.
    • Fan wiki summaries send an entry's name from our server to the wiki the game master picked; some summaries are looked up from your browser on Wikipedia.
    • The Assistant and Scan for names on maps are off unless a game master turns them on and adds their own Anthropic API key, which stays in their browser. When used, the campaign text or map picture involved is sent from that browser straight to Anthropic, under Anthropic's terms; it never passes through our server.
    • Discord channels: a game master can have a campaign post updates to a Discord channel of theirs. Only what every player may see is posted.
    • Virtual tabletop links (Foundry VTT, Owlbear Rodeo) send campaign information only to the tabletop the game master connects with their own key.
  • If the law requires it, we may disclose information to comply with a valid legal request.

A problem report sent on to our public issue tracker on GitHub leaves out who sent it and the screenshot.

Google user data

GM's Almanac's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The only Google data we receive is the basic profile described above (name, email address, account id and profile picture address), used only to sign you in, show your name to your fellow players and contact you about your account. We don't transfer it to anyone except as described on this page, and never use it for advertising.

How long we keep it, and how it's protected

Your account and campaigns are kept while you use GM's Almanac. Deleted things are gone from the live site at once; backups of the database are kept for a limited time and replaced as new ones are made. Problem reports are kept until the administrator deletes them.

The site is served only over HTTPS. Uploaded pictures and files are stored outside the public website and sent only to members of the campaign allowed to see them. Sign-in is through Google or GitHub, so we hold no passwords; API keys for tabletop links are stored only as hashes. No system is perfectly secure, but we take care to protect what you trust us with.

Your choices and rights

  • See and change it: your name and pronouns are on your Account page; what you wrote is in your campaigns, and campaigns can be exported.
  • Leave a campaign by asking its game master, or delete a campaign you run from its settings.
  • Delete your account: email privacy@gms-almanac.com from the address you sign in with. We delete your account and your personal details (name, email, profile) within 30 days and remove you from every campaign. Tell us whether to delete the campaigns you run or hand them to another game master; what you wrote in other people's campaigns stays part of their game, without your name if you ask.
  • A copy of your data, or a correction: email us and we'll send it or fix it.
  • You can also remove GM's Almanac's access in your Google account or GitHub settings at any time.

Depending on where you live (for example the EU, the UK or California), you may have further rights over your personal information, such as to object to how it is used or to complain to a data protection authority. Email us and we'll help.

Children

GM's Almanac is not meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child under 13 has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. If a change affects how we use information we already hold, we'll say so on the site before it takes effect.
